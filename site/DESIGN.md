# longyan.app design

The site should feel like the app: exact, calm, made for reading Chinese. Black pages for type and UI, and between them colour, like a Mac's wallpaper: sheets of the demo selection's own blue, violet and rose folded over a night ground. Down the page it warms, and the closing turns toward dawn.

## Color

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Night | `#000000` | Page background. |
| Ink | `#f5f5f7` | Headlines and body text on Night. |
| Graphite | `#86868b` | Secondary text, the grey half of captions. |
| Moonlight | `#a1bde3` → `#c2abe0` | The eye: its text, mostly blue and lavender, with a little rose and mint where the tint turns, like a pearl. Each character has one tint; nothing moves. |
| Tone 1–4 | `#e39e94` `#9ecca8` `#a1bde3` `#c2abe0` | The app's dark-mode tone colors. Only the cards use them, where they mean something. |

Backdrops (`paper.js`, for the statement's card, the recording and the closing) are a few sheets of colour folded over a deep night ground, the way macOS wallpapers are: each lit along its top edge, deepening below it, casting a soft shadow on the one behind, its colour drifting toward the next along its length. The palettes come from the selection's iridescent sheen (system blue, violet, rose), so the Liquid Glass over them has colour to catch: azure (blue to violet), cobalt (violet to magenta), sky (blue to cyan), indigo (violet to rose), and dawn for the closing (violet to coral). The top of each is kept deep, where text and the demo sentence sit. Sheets rise to the right in some backdrops and to the left in others, at angles of their own, with uneven edges, so no two look alike. Live backdrops drift slowly. Grain is a faint CSS overlay. Where words sit on a full-bleed backdrop (the recording's heading, the closing), a soft dark halo behind them keeps them readable over the brightest sheets.

## Type

- **SF Pro Display**, semibold (600), tight tracking, for headlines. Crisp rather than airy.
- **SF Pro Text** for body and UI. Captions lead with a bold phrase and continue in grey.
- **PingFang SC** for Chinese, everywhere.

## Layout

1. **Story.** Pinned while it plays. The Longyan eye over a range of mountains, all of it in Chinese characters. The mountains are a halftone made of characters: light ones (一, 人) where it's dim, dense ones where it's bright, none where it's dark, with mist drifting through. A few faint characters are stars. The eye is set in everyday sentences, in pearl tints, in characters of nine sizes. Every character is whole; nothing is masked or cut. On the outline a character may stand a hair past the edge (well inside the blank margin round its ink, so the outline stays crisp), so the outline takes every size from fine to large and reads as type rather than a stencil. A few small characters sit right across the point where the pupil meets the wedge, so the hook reads as one stroke, as in the icon. Characters too thin to fill their square, like 一 and 人, are left out of it. It comes in left to right. Pointing at a character shows the word it's part of and what it means in that sentence, from the app. Nothing says so: it's there to be found. Scrolling breaks the eye up, and its characters float out from all over, in arcs, into one large sentence (up to 64px on a wide screen, 40px on a phone). Most come from the eye's big characters, so you see them lift out of the shape. Selecting works as in any Mac text: drag across characters, double-click a word, triple-click the sentence, shift-click to extend. Let go and the app's card opens for what the app reads there, while the selection stays exactly where it was made, as in any app. One character is one character, and its card says which word it's part of (a click on that row opens the word's card in its place); a selection that cuts a word in half gets the card for the whole word, as the app widens it. Every selection the sentence allows has the app's own answer. No marks under the words. There is one sentence; no "Next sentence". It hands over from the canvas to selectable text in a single frame, with no fade, so nothing doubles or flashes; only the heading, "Highlight any chunk of this Chinese text.", fades in. Then it plays on its own while the page holds still for 0.8 of a screen of scrolling (the page never scrolls itself): after a beat, "Highlight" is selected the way you'd drag across it; once it's selected the sentence catches the light character by character, and an arrow in a circle after the heading points down at the sentence, nudging toward it. It steps back once you've selected something. Two bars under the sentence (72 by 5 px, as are the statement's) fill on their own: the first with the selection (1.6 s), the second while you try it (6 s). When the second is full, a button saying what comes next, "How it picks the meaning" (black, with a white outline, arrow and words, quieter than the hero's white button), comes in under them and glides down to the statement (slow, quick, slow), which opens on its first line. Scrolling back above the sentence resets it to play again. The opening glides: an even pace, easing to a stop only at the end.
2. **Statement.** Three lines, shown one at a time ("An ordinary dictionary would throw every meaning at you." with "every meaning" in the system purple / "Longyan shows you which one your sentence really means…" with just "one" in the checkmark's blue / "and teaches you the cultural context you need to know." with "cultural context" in green), beside the app's real word card for 躺平, on Liquid Glass over a small wallpaper (the same painted backdrop as the feature tiles), as it floats over a Mac's desktop. A segmented switch under it, like macOS's Appearance setting, shows the card in light (the app's frosted white tint and light colours) or dark (its smoky tint; the wallpaper dims, as macOS's do); the choice is remembered on that device. The wallpaper is tall enough for the card at its fullest, so it never resizes as the card grows in the demo sentence, built into the page. The section is pinned for three stretches of scrolling, 60vh each, one per line; three short bars under the line fill as you scroll through each stretch, so you can see how far along you are. The next line only comes once you've scrolled to the end of the last one's stretch, and never sooner than 1.2 s after it, so each line and its card change settle first; the old line lifts away and the new one rises in. The card shows a little more with each line, in that line's colour, with one light behind the meanings: first just 躺平, its pinyin and both meanings, with one purple box over both a moment after it comes in ("every" is purple too); then "to opt out of the rat race" opens in under the word as the box draws in onto that meaning on the app's spring as it turns blue, the check springs in and the other meaning steps back; then the "Cultural context" note opens in green as the blue fades. The light is a plain wash with no outline, in purple because warm hues (yellow, orange) go muddy over the dark card; each colour is its own layer, so they cross-fade without a muddy mix. Until the note is in, the card can't be clicked, so nothing breaks the sequence; after that it works like the app (and going back up resets it). Nothing in it re-wraps or snaps: it hugs its content, the space above the meaning opens with it, the pick isn't set in bold, there's no note mark, and going back up closes things smoothly. The same care holds for the card in the try section: rows restyle in place, so a pick's check springs in while the old one fades, and "Back to …" and the captions open and close. The content is set from the top, so the card grows downward and the line never moves. Scrolling back up undoes it all in order. It works the same on a phone or an upright tablet, with the card under the line; any landscape screen over 900px keeps them side by side. On a landscape screen the section is pulled up over the opening's last 35vh, so the first line and the card rise in right behind the demo sentence. Without motion the lines simply stack beside the whole card. A click on a meaning shows the whole card.
3. **Longyan works everywhere on your Mac.** Full-bleed azure backdrop, its sheets set low so the heading sits on the night. The recording fills its frame edge to edge (a screen recording with the app's own zooms, no desktop around it), with a screen's rounded corners and shadow, sized so all of it fits in the window at once. It plays muted on a loop while it's on screen (HEVC, H.264 where there's no HEVC); without motion it shows its first frame with the usual controls.
4. **Made for Chinese language learners at every level.** "Longyan brings a dictionary of nearly 200,000 words, and picks the meaning your sentence needs." Four tiles, each a piece of the real UI with a caption under it. Each tile is one colour, as bright as the page is dark, seen through the same soft pane of glass (`media/glass-1.svg`: a great disc of light, a lighter strip along the foot, a lighter pane at one side), so the four read as a set; the glass drifts slowly while a tile is on screen, mirrored on every other tile. Rose for the notes, amber for the tones, blue for History (its Export button's blue), violet for the menu (Longyan's own). Each tile does one thing, on a loop, only while on screen: real notes from the app come up one at a time, the two before stepping back behind; the four tones are said in turn, each column lit in its colour while its pitch contour draws over the character; a new word arrives on top of History every few seconds, the list running off the foot of the tile; the eye sits in a menu bar across the top, its menu centred in the tile below it, and the highlight runs down it. Still with reduced motion.
5. **Installation for macOS.** Its own thing, not a fifth tile: a Mac's screen on the black, a night wallpaper of the site's own colours drifting on it, playing the four steps as they happen: the zip opened in Finder and the app dragged to Applications; the "Not Opened" alert, and Done; System Settings › Privacy & Security scrolled down to Open Anyway; the app's own first window (its real words), Accessibility switched on, and the reading model downloading. Each app's menu bar shows that app's menus. Beside it the four steps, the one playing lit with a bar filling as it plays (as the opening's bars do); click a step to see it. It plays only on screen and goes round again. On a phone the screen shows the middle of the desk, larger. With reduced motion each step shows its scene as a still.
6. **Closing.** Full-bleed dawn backdrop, the eye mark in white with no tile around it, "Free + Offline + Open source" (the plus signs light and dimmer), one plain line ("There's no account to make and nothing to pay. All the code is on GitHub."), the download button.

## Crispness

- The mark is drawn from its geometry, not traced: three nested eye shapes (each the hull of a circle and two points, half-widths 1, 5/8 and 3/14) and one wedge. The stroke is even all round and the edges are as sharp as the screen. The old PNG wobbled.
- The tab icon is the mark in white on a black rounded square that fills the icon (`images/tab-icon.svg`, with `favicon.ico` for browsers that want one). The app icon, with its margin and shadow, is too small to read at 16px. On iPhone the home-screen icon is the same, square and opaque (`images/touch-icon.png`); iOS rounds it.
- One material: characters. Everything in the hero is drawn in them, whole. No masks, no halo, no glow.
- The selection is the system blue with an iridescent violet-and-rose sheen moving through it, sized to the selection so even one character shows it.
- On a wide screen the ridges are scaled to the screen's height, as on a phone, so they're broad ranges rather than many small bumps. They stay low under the headline and open into a valley under the eye. As the story plays they fall away and fade, as on a phone.
- On a wide screen the eye is second to the headline: about as tall as the headline and the line under it (a quarter of the width, at most 470px), level with the text.
- The bar's logo is the mark alone in white, the app icon's negative, 25px wide.
- Hairlines are 0.5px. Shadows are layered: a 0.5px ring, a tight shadow and a wide soft one.
- Boxes on the black page (the statement's wallpaper, the feature tiles, the Mac's bezel) get a light 1px rim drawn over their content, so a dark edge never melts into the page on a phone.

## Motion

- On load the mountains rise into place and the eye comes in left to right, each character flashing white as it lands. It doesn't blink.
- The story is scrubbed by scroll, smoothed a little each frame so a wheel step doesn't jump. The eye breaking up into the sentence takes three quarters of a screen of scrolling; the sentence then holds for 0.8 of a screen to try before the page moves on (255vh in all), while its own sequence plays in time, not with the scroll. "See how it works" glides down to the sentence over about two and a half seconds, slow away, quick through the middle and slow to rest, so the eye is seen breaking up into it; any scroll of your own stops the glide. The hero's "Download for Mac" glides the same way down to the closing, where the download button sits with what it needs, rather than downloading on the spot. On a phone the two are matching pills, one over the other. The headline rises line by line on load. Once the sentences form, the word underlines draw in. The card is the app's, with its springs: it scales in from the selection, its height follows its content, and rows slide open.
- Below the story, sections rise into place once, and the recording lifts out of its backdrop as it comes into view.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no scramble, no rise, the hero drawn still. `?still` does the same for screenshots.

## The card

The demo card is the app's card, not a picture of it: same sizes, colors, Liquid Glass and motion, and the same answers. When the app's card changes, `card.js` changes with it. Pin works as it does in the app: a pinned card stays where it is, a new lookup replaces it in place, and only its close button or Esc closes it (it goes when the sentence does). The card built into the statement has no Pin.

## Words

Plain and short. Say what the app does in the visitor's words. No "seamless", "powerful", "unlock". Buttons say what happens: "Download for Mac".
