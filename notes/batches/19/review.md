## Notes

- **还好** [hái hǎo] *not bad; pretty good* · lost in translation  
  As a reply, usually lukewarm: 还好吧 means "it's okay", short of praise. A polite way to avoid saying something is good or bad.
- **发炎** [fā yán] *to become inflamed* · lost in translation  
  An everyday word, not a clinical one: 嗓子发炎 is a sore throat and 伤口发炎 is an infected cut.
- **安插** [ān chā] *to assign to a job* · lost in translation  
  Usually disapproving: 安插亲信 means slotting one's own people into posts through connections.
- **安插** [ān chā] *to plant* · lost in translation  
  Used for spies and informers placed in secret: 安插眼线 ("plant an informer"), 安插卧底 ("plant a mole").
- **总结** [zǒng jié] *summary* · cultural context  
  A standard report workers and students are expected to write: 年终总结 is the year-end review of one's own work, often required by employers.
- **转正** [zhuǎn zhèng] *to transfer to full membership* · cultural context  
  At work, passing probation to become a regular employee. In the Communist Party, a probationary member becoming a full member.
- **不在** [bù zài] *(euphemism) to pass away* · lost in translation  
  Usually with 了: 他已经不在了 ("he's no longer with us"). A gentle way to mention a death, common in speech.
- **五险** [wǔ xiǎn] *the five social insurances (pension, medical, unemployment, work injury and maternity)* · cultural context  
  Employers are legally required to pay these, usually mentioned as 五险一金 with the housing fund added. Job ads list it as a basic benefit.
- **什么人** [shén me rén] *what (kind of) person?* · lost in translation  
  Can sound like a challenge: 你是什么人？ demands to know someone's standing. 他是你什么人？ asks how someone is related to the listener.
- **办理** [bàn lǐ] *to handle; to transact; to conduct* · lost in translation  
  The standard verb for paperwork at banks and government offices: 办理手续 ("go through the formalities"), 办理业务.
- **吃饭** [chī fàn] *to have a meal* · cultural context  
  请你吃饭 means the speaker is paying. Older people still greet with 吃饭了吗？ as a hello rather than a question.
- **吃饭** [chī fàn] *to make a living* · lost in translation  
  Usually in 靠……吃饭: 靠手艺吃饭 ("live by one's craft"), 靠脸吃饭 ("get by on looks").
- **嘀咕** [dí gu] *to feel apprehensive* · lost in translation  
  Usually 心里嘀咕 or 犯嘀咕: having doubts or misgivings that one keeps to oneself.
- **回家** [huí jiā] *to return home* · cultural context  
  At New Year, 回家 usually means travelling back to one's hometown and parents: 回家过年.
- **学历** [xué lì] *educational background* · cultural context  
  The highest level of schooling completed (本科, 硕士), which employers screen on closely. Separate from 学位, the degree actually awarded.
- **教养** [jiào yǎng] *upbringing; breeding; culture* · lost in translation  
  Mostly about manners. 没教养 is a strong insult that reflects on how someone's parents raised them.
- **普通话** [pǔ tōng huà] *Putonghua; standard Mandarin* · cultural context  
  The official name in mainland China for standard Mandarin. Taiwan calls it 国语 and Singapore 华语.
- **沉魚落雁** [chén yú luò yàn] *lit. fish sink, goose alights (idiom, from Zhuangzi 莊子|庄子[Zhuang1 zi3]); fig. female beauty captivating even the birds and beasts* · lost in translation  
  A classical compliment for a woman's beauty, often paired with 闭月羞花. Later stories link it to the ancient beauties Xi Shi and Wang Zhaojun.
- **派购** [pài gòu] *fixed government purchase (esp of farm products)* · cultural context  
  A planned-economy term: the state set quotas of grain, pigs and other farm goods that producers had to sell to it at fixed prices.
- **流浪** [liú làng] *homeless* · lost in translation  
  Also the usual word for strays: 流浪猫 and 流浪狗 are stray cats and dogs. 流浪汉 is a homeless man.
- **玉女** [yù nǚ] *beautiful woman* · lost in translation  
  In entertainment news, a young actress or singer with a pure, innocent image: 玉女明星.
- **瓜分** [guā fēn] *to partition* · lost in translation  
  Suggests greedy carving up by stronger parties, as when foreign powers sought to 瓜分中国 in the late Qing. Apps also use it for prize pools: 瓜分百万红包.
- **脸色** [liǎn sè] *look* · lost in translation  
  看人脸色 means having to watch someone's moods to stay in their favour; 给人脸色看 means showing displeasure through one's expression.
- **调休** [tiáo xiū] *to compensate for working on a holiday by resting on a workday* · cultural context  
  Part of the official holiday system, where weekends are turned into workdays to make longer breaks. The practice draws frequent complaints.
- **顶替** [dǐng tì] *to replace* · lost in translation  
  Often means taking someone's place under false pretences: 冒名顶替. Cases of people taking others' university places this way have caused public outrage.
- **百姓** [bǎi xìng] *common people* · cultural context  
  Ordinary people as opposed to officials. 老百姓 is how people describe themselves in contrast to those in power, and officials use it too.
- **功夫** [gōng fu] *effort* · lost in translation  
  Also means time: 没功夫 ("no time"), 一会儿功夫 ("a moment"). 下功夫 means putting real effort into something.
- **老师** [lǎo shī] *teacher* · lost in translation  
  Also a polite title for people in the arts, media and other fields who don't teach: 王老师 can be a TV host or an editor.
- **方便** [fāng biàn] *convenient; suitable* · lost in translation  
  方便吗？ asks whether now is a good time, for a call or a visit. 不方便 is a polite way to decline without giving a reason.
- **有点** [yǒu diǎn] *a little* · lost in translation  
  Mostly comes before something unwelcome: 有点贵 ("a bit pricey"), 有点累 ("a bit tired").
- **应该** [yīng gāi] *ought to; should; must* · lost in translation  
  Often means "probably": 应该没问题 ("it should be fine"). 应该的 is a modest reply to thanks: "it was the least I could do".
- **漂亮** [piào liang] *pretty; beautiful* · lost in translation  
  Also praises things done well: 干得漂亮 ("nicely done"). 说漂亮话 means saying nice-sounding things without meaning them.
- **不行** [bù xíng] *be no good* · lost in translation  
  After an adjective it means "extremely": 累得不行 ("exhausted"). 他快不行了 can mean he is dying.
- **起义** [qǐ yì] *uprising* · cultural context  
  An approving word in official histories: 农民起义 ("peasant uprising"). Rebellions seen as illegitimate are called 叛乱 instead.
- **志愿** [zhì yuàn] *aspiration* · cultural context  
  After the gaokao, 填志愿 means listing one's preferred universities and majors in ranked order; 第一志愿 is the first choice.
- **公主** [gōng zhǔ] *princess* · lost in translation  
  公主病 describes a spoiled young woman who expects to be pampered. In KTV venues 公主 is also a euphemism for a hostess.
- **备案** [bèi àn] *to put on record* · cultural context  
  Often a required registration with the authorities: websites in mainland China must display an ICP 备案 number.
- **审查** [shěn chá] *censorship* · cultural context  
  Covers official review of books, films, games and online posts, before or after release. 过审 means passing it.
- **说法** [shuō fa] *one's version (of events)* · lost in translation  
  讨个说法 means pressing those responsible, often officials or a company, to explain and make things right.
- **进士** [jìn shì] *successful candidate in the highest imperial civil service examination* · cultural context  
  The top degree of the imperial examinations, decided by the palace exam in the capital, and the usual route to high office.
- **爷爷** [yé ye] *(coll.) paternal grandfather; grandpa* · lost in translation  
  Children also call any elderly man 爷爷, and 老爷爷 is a polite way to refer to an old man in general.
- **奶奶** [nǎi nai] *(informal) grandma (paternal grandmother)* · lost in translation  
  Children also call any elderly woman 奶奶, and 老奶奶 is a polite way to refer to an old woman in general.
- **资产阶级** [zī chǎn jiē jí] *the capitalist class* · cultural context  
  In Mao-era politics a label of attack, as in 资产阶级思想 ("bourgeois thinking"). 小资, short for 小资产阶级, now means someone with refined middle-class tastes.
- **估计** [gū jì] *(coll.) to reckon; to think (that ...)* · lost in translation  
  In speech it often just means "probably": 估计他不来了, "he's probably not coming".
- **爱人** [ài ren] *spouse (PRC)* · cultural context  
  Long the standard mainland word for husband or wife. Younger speakers more often say 老公 or 老婆, and forms use 配偶.
- **大爷** [dà yé] *arrogant idler* · lost in translation  
  Someone who acts like a lord expecting to be served. 别把自己当大爷 means "stop acting like you own the place".
- **大爷** [dà ye] *term of respect for older man* · lost in translation  
  The usual way to address an old man in the north, stranger or not: 大爷，去车站怎么走？ "Excuse me, sir, how do I get to the station?"
- **大妈** [dà mā] *aunt (affectionate term for an elderly woman)* · lost in translation  
  The usual way to address a middle-aged or older woman in the north. In the media, 中国大妈 is a collective label for such women, as shoppers or square dancers.
- **吃醋** [chī cù] *to feel jealous (esp. because of a rival in love or affection)* · lost in translation  
  Literally "eat vinegar". Often light and teasing, said of partners or of a child jealous of a new sibling.
- **坐月子** [zuò yuè zi] *to convalesce for a month following childbirth, following a special diet, and observing various taboos to protect the body from exposure to the "wind"* · cultural context  
  Still widely observed: new mothers are often told not to wash their hair, bathe or go outdoors. Paid 月子中心 (postpartum centres) are common in cities.
- **喜酒** [xǐ jiǔ] *wedding feast* · cultural context  
  喝喜酒 means attending a wedding. 什么时候请我们喝喜酒？ is a teasing way of asking someone when they will get married.
- **份子钱** [fèn zi qián] *gift money (on the occasion of a wedding etc)* · cultural context  
  Cash given at weddings and similar events, with the amount set by closeness and local custom. Rising amounts are a common complaint.
- **压岁钱** [yā suì qián] *money given to children as a gift on Chinese New Year's Eve* · cultural context  
  Given by elders in red envelopes. Parents often take it to keep "for later", a long-running family joke.
- **月饼** [yuè bǐng] *mooncake (esp. for the Mid-Autumn Festival)* · cultural context  
  Given as gifts to family, colleagues and clients before the festival. Lavish gift boxes have drawn official criticism for extravagance.
- **粽子** [zòng zi] *glutinous rice and choice of filling wrapped in leaves and steamed or boiled* · cultural context  
  The food of the Dragon Boat Festival. Whether the filling should be sweet or savoury is a running north-south argument online.
- **算命** [suàn mìng] *fortune-telling* · cultural context  
  Often based on one's birth date and hour (八字). Some families still consult a fortune-teller about marriage matches or wedding dates.
- **本命年** [běn mìng nián] *year of one's birth sign, according to the cycle of 12 animals of the earthly branches 地支[di4 zhi1]* · cultural context  
  Traditionally an unlucky year, so people wear red underwear, belts or bracelets to ward off bad luck.
- **黄牛** [huáng niú] *scalper of tickets etc* · lost in translation  
  Covers scalpers of train tickets, concert tickets and hospital appointment slips. A gang of them is 黄牛党.
- **山寨** [shān zhài] *(fig.) knockoff (goods)* · cultural context  
  Used for cheap clones of brand phones and then for any knockoff or parody. Can be playful as well as dismissive: 山寨版 ("knockoff version").
- **土豪** [tǔ háo] *local tyrant* · cultural context  
  In revolutionary language, a rural landlord or strongman; the slogan 打土豪，分田地 called for seizing their land.
- **土豪** [tǔ háo] *(slang) nouveau riche* · lost in translation  
  Someone with lots of money and showy taste. Often joking: 土豪，我们做朋友吧 ("rich guy, let's be friends").
- **高富帅** [gāo fù shuài] *"Mr Perfect" (i.e. tall, rich and handsome) (Internet slang)* · lost in translation  
  An online label for the ideal suitor, often used mockingly, and set against the self-mocking 屌丝 ("loser").
- **白富美** [bái fù měi] *"Ms Perfect" (i.e. fair-skinned, rich and beautiful) (Internet slang)* · lost in translation  
  The female counterpart of 高富帅. 白 is there because pale skin is widely prized as a beauty standard.
- **打卡** [dǎ kǎ] *(on social media) to check in to a location* · lost in translation  
  Also visiting a trendy place to take photos and post them: 网红打卡地 ("a spot famous online"). Daily habit check-ins are called 打卡 too.
- **网红** [wǎng hóng] *(attributive) popular on the Internet* · lost in translation  
  网红店 and 网红餐厅 are places made popular online, often with long queues. The label can suggest style over substance.
- **种草** [zhòng cǎo] *(neologism) (slang) to spark interest in a product or idea (esp. on social media)* · lost in translation  
  Shopping slang from social media: 被种草 means being made to want something, and 拔草 means buying it or losing interest.
- **吃瓜** [chī guā] *(neologism c. 2016) (slang) to watch an entertaining spectacle from the sidelines and-or engage in gossip about it* · lost in translation  
  From 吃瓜群众 ("melon-eating masses"), bystanders following celebrity scandals online. Said of oneself, it signals curiosity without taking sides.
- **杠精** [gàng jīng] *(slang) (neologism c. 2018) argumentative person* · lost in translation  
  An online insult for someone who argues with everything for its own sake, from 抬杠 ("to bicker").
- **老铁** [lǎo tiě] *(slang) very close friend; bro* · lost in translation  
  Northeastern slang spread by livestreaming apps, where hosts call viewers 老铁. 老铁，没毛病 means "bro, no problem".
- **小鲜肉** [xiǎo xiān ròu] *(coll.) teen idol (male)* · lost in translation  
  Literally "little fresh meat": a young, good-looking male star. Can be admiring or a jab at young actors seen as all looks and no skill.
- **富二代** [fù èr dài] *children of entrepreneurs who became wealthy under Deng Xiaoping's economic reforms in the 1980s* · cultural context  
  Now used of any rich family's children, usually with an image of entitlement and showy spending.
- **官二代** [guān èr dài] *children of officials* · cultural context  
  Common in stories about privilege. The 我爸是李刚 ("my dad is Li Gang") case made it a byword for using a parent's power to escape consequences.
- **拼爹** [pīn diē] *(slang) to rely on one's father's wealth or prestige to get ahead* · lost in translation  
  Said resentfully of competition decided by family background, not effort: 这年头还得拼爹 ("these days it comes down to who your dad is").
- **啃老** [kěn lǎo] *(coll.) to live with and depend on one's parents even upon reaching adulthood* · cultural context  
  Literally "gnaw on the old", and disapproving. 啃老族 is the label for adults who live off their parents.
- **裸婚** [luǒ hūn] *no-frills civil wedding ceremony lacking a material foundation: no car, house, reception, rings, or honeymoon* · cultural context  
  Marrying without the flat, car and big wedding that families often expect. Can be a stand against those expectations or simply what a couple can afford.
- **房奴** [fáng nú] *a slave to one's mortgage* · cultural context  
  A common self-description among urban homeowners paying decades-long mortgages. Owning a flat is widely seen as a precondition for marriage.
- **学区房** [xué qū fáng] *a residential property located within the designated catchment area of a desirable school, often sought after by parents to secure school admission for their children* · cultural context  
  These flats can cost far more than homes nearby, since public school places depend on where a family owns its home.
- **外卖** [wài mài] *takeout (meal)* · cultural context  
  Mostly ordered through apps such as Meituan; 点外卖 ("order delivery") is an everyday habit, and couriers are called 外卖小哥.
- **朋友圈** [Péng you quān] *Moments (social networking function of smartphone app WeChat 微信[Wei1 xin4])* · cultural context  
  The main place people post personal updates: 发朋友圈 ("post on Moments"). Hiding posts from parents or bosses is common.
- **碰瓷** [pèng cí] *(coll.) to scam sb by setting up an "accident" in which one appears to have sustained damage or injury caused by the scam victim, then demanding compensation (variations include putting "expensive" porcelain in a place where it is likely to be knocked over by passers-by, and stepping into the path of a slow-moving car)* · lost in translation  
  Also used figuratively for riding on someone else's fame or picking a fight for attention: 碰瓷名人 ("leech off celebrities").
- **城管** [chéng guǎn] *local government bylaw enforcement officer* · cultural context  
  Officers who clear unlicensed street vendors and enforce city rules. Clashes with vendors have given them a rough public image.
- **上访** [shàng fǎng] *to seek an audience with higher-ups (esp. government officials) to petition for sth* · cultural context  
  Citizens take grievances to higher levels of government through the 信访 ("letters and visits") system, often travelling to provincial capitals or Beijing.
- **拆迁** [chāi qiān] *to demolish a building and relocate the inhabitants* · cultural context  
  Widespread during urban redevelopment. Compensation can make families rich, hence 拆二代, but forced evictions are a common source of disputes.
- **钉子户** [dīng zi hù] *householder who refuses to vacate his home despite pressure from property developers* · lost in translation  
  Literally "nail household", one that can't be pulled out. Also used of any holdout who refuses to budge.
- **小三** [xiǎo sān] *mistress* · lost in translation  
  Blunt and disapproving, aimed at the woman blamed for breaking up a marriage. 打小三 means confronting her, often in public.
- **二奶** [èr nǎi] *mistress* · cultural context  
  A kept mistress supported with money or a flat, an arrangement long associated with wealthy businessmen and officials.
- **韭菜** [jiǔ cài] *(coll.) naive or easily exploited people (esp. small investors or consumers), likened to chives that can be repeatedly harvested for profit* · lost in translation  
  Investors' slang: 割韭菜 ("harvest the chives") is fleecing small investors again and again, and losers call themselves 韭菜.
- **背锅** [bēi guō] *(slang) (neologism derived from 背黑鍋|背黑锅[bei1 hei1 guo1]) to carry the can; to be a scapegoat* · lost in translation  
  Office and online slang for taking the blame for someone else's mistake; 背锅侠 is the person who always ends up blamed.
- **甩锅** [shuǎi guō] *(neologism, attested by 2017) to shift the blame; to pass the buck* · lost in translation  
  Common in office talk and political commentary. The opposite of 背锅, and the two often appear together.
- **套路** [tào lù] *routine* · lost in translation  
  Now often a trick or manipulative script: 别跟我玩套路 ("don't play games with me"), 套路太深 ("too many tricks").
- **心机** [xīn jī] *scheme* · lost in translation  
  Mostly disapproving in modern use: 有心机 or 心机女 describes someone calculating and manipulative.
- **走心** [zǒu xīn] *to take care* · lost in translation  
  Common slang for doing something with real care: 这礼物很走心 ("a thoughtful gift"). 不走心 means half-hearted.
- **打酱油** [dǎ jiàng yóu] *it's none of my business ("I’m just here to buy some soy sauce")* · lost in translation  
  A catchphrase for being a mere bystander or having a minor role: 我是来打酱油的 ("I'm just passing through").
- **雷人** [léi rén] *(Internet slang) shocking* · lost in translation  
  Online slang for something so bizarre or tasteless it stuns, like being struck by lightning: 雷人的造型 ("an outrageous outfit").
- **坑爹** [kēng diē] *(vulgar) (of sth that makes one feel misled or let down etc) to be a big disappointment* · lost in translation  
  An online exclamation for a letdown or rip-off: 这电影太坑爹了 ("this film is such a letdown"). Crude-sounding but widely used.
- **萌** [méng] *(coll.) cute; adorable (orthographic borrowing from Japanese 萌え "moe", affection for an anime or manga character)* · lost in translation  
  Online it covers anything cute, including adults acting cute (卖萌). 萌萌哒 is a gushing way of saying adorable.
- **宅男** [zhái nán] *a guy who stays at home all the time, typically spending a lot of time playing online games (derived from Japanese "otaku")* · lost in translation  
  Can be self-mocking or dismissive. It usually means an introverted homebody, not specifically an anime fan.
- **粉丝** [fěn sī] *fan; enthusiast for sb or sth (loanword from "fans")* · lost in translation  
  Also the follower count on social media: 粉丝多 ("lots of followers"). A star's fans often go by a collective nickname.
- **饭圈** [fàn quān] *fan community* · cultural context  
  Organised celebrity fandom that buys in bulk, boosts rankings and attacks critics online. Authorities have cracked down on its excesses.
- **小姐姐** [xiǎo jiě jie] *(neologism c. 2017) (slang) young lady (amiable form of address for a young woman of about one's own age or a little older)* · lost in translation  
  A friendly, slightly cute word for a young woman, common online and with strangers. Often preferred to 小姐, which can suggest a sex worker.
- **广场舞** [guǎng chǎng wǔ] *square dancing, an exercise routine performed to music in public squares, parks and plazas, popular esp. among middle-aged and retired women in China* · cultural context  
  Loud music from groups of dancers is a common neighbourhood complaint, and disputes over noise and space make regular news.
- **熊孩子** [xióng hái zi] *(dialect) little devil; brat* · lost in translation  
  Used in exasperation, often online about children who damage things in public, with the parents blamed as 熊家长.
- **妈宝** [mā bǎo] *mama's boy* · lost in translation  
  Also 妈宝男. A sharp criticism in dating talk of a man who defers to his mother in everything, including his marriage.
- **凤凰男** [fèng huáng nán] *(Internet slang) guy who grew up in the countryside and gained a foothold in the city through hard work* · cultural context  
  Often critical in marriage talk, implying he puts his rural family's demands before his urban wife. Contrasted with 孔雀女, a city-raised woman.
- **剩男** [shèng nán] *(coll.) "leftover man" – a man who remains single beyond the typical marrying age* · cultural context  
  Less of an insult than 剩女. It often points to rural men unable to find wives because of the gender imbalance.
- **丁克** [dīng kè] *Dual Income, No Kids (DINK) (loanword)* · cultural context  
  Couples who choose this often face pressure from parents. The word is used as a self-label and in debates about falling birth rates.
- **独生子女** [dú shēng zǐ nǚ] *an only child* · cultural context  
  The generation born under the one-child policy, in force from about 1980 to 2015. Media often stereotype them as spoiled 小皇帝 ("little emperors").
- **计划生育** [jì huà shēng yù] *family planning* · cultural context  
  The official name for state birth limits, chiefly the one-child policy, run by local family planning offices. Not a private choice about contraception.
- **留守儿童** [liú shǒu ér tóng] *"left-behind children", rural children whose parents have to make a living as migrant workers in distant urban areas, but cannot afford to keep the family with them* · cultural context  
  Usually raised by grandparents in the home village. A recognised social problem, regularly covered in news and government policy.
- **空巢** [kōng cháo] *empty nest* · cultural context  
  Mostly heard in 空巢老人, elderly people living alone because their children work elsewhere, a common topic in news on ageing.
- **小康** [xiǎo kāng] *moderately affluent* · cultural context  
  Best known from 小康社会 ("moderately prosperous society"), the long-running official development goal, declared achieved in 2021.
- **走亲戚** [zǒu qīn qi] *to visit relatives* · cultural context  
  Mostly done at Spring Festival, when families go round relatives' homes in turn, bringing gifts.
- **拜年** [bài nián] *to pay a New Year call* · cultural context  
  Done in person, by phone or by message during Spring Festival. Children who 拜年 to elders expect a 红包 in return.
- **守岁** [shǒu suì] *to stay up all night on lunar New Year's Eve* · cultural context  
  A family custom of staying up past midnight together on New Year's Eve, now often in front of the 春晚 TV gala.
- **年夜饭** [nián yè fàn] *reunion dinner on Lunar New Year's Eve (Chinese New Year tradition)* · cultural context  
  The most important family meal of the year; people travel across the country to be home for it. Fish is served for 年年有余 ("surplus every year").
