## Notes

- **人家** [rén jia] *other people* · lost in translation  
  Other people, usually with a sting. 你看人家 compares you to someone doing better, and you've heard it from your parents your whole life.
- **人家** [rén jia] *I, me (referring to oneself as "one" or "people")* · lost in translation  
  I, me, said coyly. When someone calls herself 人家 she's being cute or sulky, as in 人家不要嘛.
- **姑娘** [gū niang] *daughter* · lost in translation  
  A daughter, said warmly. 我家姑娘 sounds fonder than 女儿, the way a proud parent talks about her to neighbors.
- **积极** [jī jí] *active* · cultural context  
  Eager and keen. At school and work 积极 is praise from above, and an 积极分子 is someone on the way to joining the Party, so it can also tease a person for trying too hard.
- **物质** [wù zhì] *materialistic* · lost in translation  
  Materialistic. Saying a partner is 很物质 means they care about money and what you can buy them, and it's rarely a compliment.
- **身体** [shēn tǐ] *one's health* · cultural context  
  Your health. 注意身体 is how you sign off with elders, and asking 身体好吗 is a way of showing you care.
- **特色** [tè sè] *a characteristic; a distinctive feature or quality* · cultural context  
  A distinctive feature. You hear 中国特色 in official speech, and you also use it half-jokingly to explain anything that only makes sense here.
- **人才** [rén cái] *talented person* · lost in translation  
  A talented person. 真是个人才 is often a tease, said when someone does something absurdly clever or just absurd.
- **母亲** [mǔ qīn] *mother* · lost in translation  
  Mother, formally. You call yours 妈, and 母亲 is for writing and speeches, so it feels solemn and respectful.
