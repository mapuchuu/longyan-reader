## Notes

- **可以** [kě yǐ] *not bad* · lost in translation  
  Pretty good, said as a verdict. When a friend says 你可以啊, they're impressed and teasing you a little, and 还可以 just means it's fine.
- **人民** [rén mín] *the people* · cultural context  
  The people, as the state speaks of them. You see it on money, in 人民日报 and in 为人民服务, so it sounds solemn and official, never just a crowd.
- **文化** [wén huà] *culture* · lost in translation  
  Culture, and also plain schooling. When someone says 没文化 or 文化不高, you know they mean little education, so it can sting or be a modest shrug.
- **研究** [yán jiū] *to look into* · lost in translation  
  To look into something, and in an office, to put it off. 我们研究研究 can be a polite way of telling you nothing will happen soon.
- **组织** [zǔ zhī] *organization* · cultural context  
  An organisation, and in political talk the Party itself. When you hear 相信组织 or 组织上决定, you know who is meant, and it can be solemn or half a joke.
- **一般** [yī bān] *so-so* · lost in translation  
  So-so, and often a polite way of saying not good. When you say the food is 一般, the cook should hear the disappointment.
- **机关** [jī guān] *office; agency; organ; organization; establishment; institution; body* · cultural context  
  A government or Party office. You picture a secure desk, tea and paperwork, and parents still hope their children will land a job in one.
- **政治** [zhèng zhì] *politics* · cultural context  
  Politics, and also a subject everyone is examined on at school. You remember memorising it for exams, and 讲政治 means loyalty to the line more than debate.
- **主席** [zhǔ xí] *chairman* · cultural context  
  A chairman of any body, from a student union up. Said alone, though, you hear Mao, and 毛主席 is still how most people say his name.
- **教育** [jiào yù] *to educate* · lost in translation  
  To educate, and also to give someone a talking-to. When a boss 教育 you, you're being scolded for your own good, and you're expected to take it.
- **改革** [gǎi gé] *reform* · cultural context  
  Reform, and for most people the reforms that began in 1978. You hear 改革开放 and think of how much your family's life changed.
- **我国** [wǒ guó] *my country; our country* · lost in translation  
  Our country, meaning China. Reports and textbooks use it as the default, so you can hear the voice of a newspaper or an official in it.
- **有关** [yǒu guān] *related to* · cultural context  
  Related or concerned. You know 有关部门 best, the "relevant departments" that are never named, and you shrug because nobody can tell you who decided.
