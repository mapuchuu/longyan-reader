- **意见** [yì jiàn] *objection* · lost in translation  
  A complaint, a grudge. 我对他有意见 doesn't mean you have thoughts about someone, it means you're unhappy with them.
- **土地** [tǔ di] *local god* · cultural context  
  The local earth god. You pass small shrines to 土地公 in villages and leave offerings so he watches over the neighborhood.
- **兄弟** [xiōng dì] *brothers* · lost in translation  
  Brothers, and also your closest friends. Calling a friend 好兄弟 says you'd show up for him the way family would.
- **兄弟** [xiōng dì] *I, me (humble term used by men in public speech)* · lost in translation  
  I, me, from a man addressing a group. 兄弟我 sounds modest and rough-and-ready, like one of the guys.
- **事业** [shì yè] *publicly funded institution, enterprise or foundation* · cultural context  
  A state-funded institution like a school or hospital. A job at a 事业单位 makes you think of stability and a pension, and your parents are proud of it.
- **事业** [shì yè] *career* · lost in translation  
  Your career as a life's work. 事业有成 means more than having a job, it means you've built something people respect.
- **农民** [nóng mín] *peasant; farmer* · cultural context  
  A farmer, and a social class as much as a job. You know 农民 can be thrown as an insult for someone unsophisticated, and it stings because of the rural and urban divide.
- **工人** [gōng rén] *worker* · cultural context  
  A worker, once the honored class. If your parents had a factory job, 工人 carries the memory of a secure 铁饭碗 that many lost in the 1990s layoffs.
- **还是** [hái shi] *had better* · lost in translation  
  Had better, after weighing it up. 还是走吧 sounds like you've thought it over and settled on this.
- **根本** [gēn běn] *(not) at all* · lost in translation

  Correct, textbook. When you tell someone their 普通话 is 很标准, it's real praise, and they're pleased to hear it.
