## Notes

- **手续** [shǒu xù] *formalities* · cultural context  
  The paperwork and stamps that make something official. You picture forms, queues and being sent from one window to the next until every box is signed.
- **一线** [yī xiàn] *front line; front-line position* · cultural context  
  The front line, where the actual work gets done. 一线员工 sounds respectful with a military echo, and you also hear it in 一线城市 for the biggest cities.
- **严打** [yán dǎ] *to crack down on* · cultural context  
  A strike-hard police campaign, famously from 1983 on. You picture sudden mass arrests and fast, harsh sentences, and older people still remember the fear.
- **临时工** [lín shí gōng] *temporary worker; temp* · cultural context  
  A worker hired short-term, with little security. When an official scandal breaks and the blame lands on a 临时工, you roll your eyes, because it's become a national joke.
- **义务** [yì wù] *volunteer (work etc)* · lost in translation  
  Done for free, as a kind of duty. 义务劳动 brings back school and work-unit days, when you "volunteered" because you were told to.
- **五湖四海** [wǔ hú sì hǎi] *all parts of the country* · lost in translation  
  Every corner of the country. You hear warmth and togetherness in it: strangers from all over, thrown together and getting along.
- **争取** [zhēng qǔ] *to strive for* · lost in translation  
  To try hard to win or reach something. When you say 我争取明天交, it's a soft promise: you'll do your best, so don't hold me to it.
- **便宜** [pián yi] *a petty advantage* · lost in translation  
  A small gain at someone else's expense. When someone 占你便宜, you feel quietly cheated, and the phrase can also mean taking liberties with a woman.
- **八卦** [bā guà] *the eight divinatory trigrams of the Book of Changes 易經|易经[Yi4 jing1]* · cultural context  
  The eight symbols of solid and broken lines from the Book of Changes. You picture them circling yin and yang on a Daoist robe or a fortune teller's table.
- **八卦** [bā guà] *gossip* · cultural context  
  Gossip, especially about celebrities, a sense that spread from Hong Kong. Calling a friend 好八卦 is teasing, and you lean in as you say it.
- **公车** [gōng chē] *car belonging to an organization and used by its members (government car, police car, company car etc)* · cultural context  
  A car owned by a government office or company. You hear 公车私用 and picture an official's black sedan at the supermarket, a long-running public complaint.
- **主食** [zhǔ shí] *staple food* · cultural context  
  The rice, noodles or buns that fill you up at a meal. Without any, you feel you haven't really eaten, however many dishes were on the table.
- **体检** [tǐ jiǎn] *abbr. for 體格檢查|体格检查[ti3 ge2 jian3 cha2]* · lost in translation  
  A full medical check-up. You expect one before starting many jobs, and an annual 体检 from your employer counts as a perk.
- **何必** [hé bì] *there is no need* · lost in translation  
  Why bother, asked with no wish for an answer. When you sigh 何必呢, you're gently telling someone it isn't worth the trouble.
