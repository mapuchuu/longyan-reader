## Notes

- **大陆** [Dà lù] *mainland China (reference to the PRC)* · cultural context  
  Mainland China. Said in Taiwan or Hong Kong it marks where you're standing, and on the mainland you hardly use it for your own home.
- **身子** [shēn zi] *pregnancy* · lost in translation  
  A pregnancy, said in an old or rural way. 她有身子了 sounds like your grandmother telling you the news quietly.
- **分子** [fèn zǐ] *member of a class or group; people of a certain type* · cultural context  
  A member of a type of group, often one under suspicion. 坏分子 and 不法分子 sound like labels on a file, and you still hear the Mao-era tone in them.
- **反映** [fǎn yìng] *(fig.) to report* · cultural context  
  To report something up. When you 反映问题 you take a complaint to someone in charge, and you choose your words carefully.
- **难道** [nán dào] *don't tell me ...* · lost in translation  
  Don't tell me. 难道 shows you don't believe it, and you're really asking the other person to admit it can't be so.
- **夫人** [fū ren] *Mrs.* · lost in translation  
  Someone's wife, said respectfully. You ask after 您夫人 politely, and you'd only call your own wife 夫人 as a joke.
- **以为** [yǐ wéi] *to think; to believe (often with the implication that the belief is mistaken – unless referring to one's own current belief)* · lost in translation  
  To think, and it usually turns out wrong. 我以为他走了 already tells you that he hadn't left.
- **社区** [shè qū] *neighborhood* · cultural context  
  Your neighborhood, as an office. The 社区 stamps your paperwork and knocks on your door, and you remember how much it ran your life during Covid.
- **成绩** [chéng jì] *grades* · cultural context  
  Grades. It's the first thing relatives ask you about at New Year, and you feel the question coming before they sit down.
- **反映** [fǎn yìng] *to make known* · cultural context  
  To report something up. When you 反映问题 you take a complaint to someone in charge, and you choose your words carefully.
- **难道** [nán dào] *could it be that...?* · lost in translation  
  Don't tell me. 难道 shows you don't believe it, and you're really asking the other person to admit it can't be so.
