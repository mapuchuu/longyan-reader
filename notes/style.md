# How nuance notes are written

A note sits under a meaning the card already shows. It adds the one thing an English
reader would miss: how the word is really used, by whom, and in what tone. Short and
plain, like a good learner's dictionary usage note.

## Do

- Lead with the useful fact. The card's headline already gives the meaning, so don't
  open by restating it; open with what the headline can't say.
- Say who uses it and when: in offices, online, to children, in official language,
  sarcastically, as an insult.
- Give a short real phrase in Chinese with its English when it shows the point better
  than a description: 我们研究研究 ("we'll look into it").
- One or two sentences. Under about 160 characters when possible; one sentence is fine.
- Plain words. State things; don't hedge or decorate.

## Don't

- Start a sentence with "You", or put the reader in an imagined scene ("You picture…",
  "You hear…", "you feel…", "Running into an old friend, you smile…").
- Describe feelings the reader is assumed to share ("You grew up with…").
- End on a neat aphorism ("never rich, never fired", "is the whole point").
- Use soft emotional filler: warm, stings, quietly, faint, a wink, a small bow.
- Talk about translation, English, or "cultural gaps". Never use em dashes.

## Examples

These set the voice. Before → after.

| Word | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| 研究 | To look into something, and in an office, to put it off. 我们研究研究 can be a polite way of telling you nothing will happen soon. | In an office, 我们研究研究 ("we'll look into it") often means no, or not soon. |
| 小人 | A petty, scheming person. You picture a coworker who smiles to your face and undermines you behind your back. | Someone who schemes against others behind their back. A strong insult; 防小人 means watching out for backstabbers. |
| 铁饭碗 | A job you can't lose, usually in the state sector. Parents dream of it for their children: never rich, never fired. | A secure job, usually in the state sector. Many parents still prefer one for their children over better-paid private work. |
| 干杯 | Cheers! Literally "dry the cup", and you're expected to finish it, not sip. | Literally "dry the cup": the glass is meant to be emptied, not sipped. |
| 红包 (gift) | A red envelope of money given at New Year, weddings and other happy occasions. Children collect them from relatives, and adults feel the pressure of giving the right amount. | Cash in a red envelope, given at New Year, weddings and birthdays. The amount is expected to suit the occasion and the relationship. |
| 红包 (bribe) | A cash gift slipped to a doctor or official for better treatment. Everyone knows what it means when a 红包 changes hands before surgery. | A cash bribe, typically to a doctor or official in return for better treatment. |
| 剩女 | A "leftover woman": single and over about 27. The label stings, and many women have taken it back with defiant humour. | A dismissive label for a woman still single in her late twenties. Now often used critically or ironically. |
| 正能量 | Positivity, especially the upbeat content officials and media promote. Calling a story 正能量 can be sincere praise or a wink at propaganda. | Upbeat content of the kind state media promotes. Can be sincere praise or a dig at propaganda. |
| 劳动 | Work, usually with your hands, spoken of as something honourable. You grew up with the slogan 劳动最光荣 and with school chores called 劳动, so it sounds weightier than plain work. | Manual work, which official language treats as honourable: 劳动最光荣, "labour is most glorious". School chores are also called 劳动. |
| 好意思 | To feel comfortable doing something you arguably shouldn't. You mostly hear it as an accusation, 你还好意思说, where the shame someone ought to feel is the whole point. | Mostly heard in accusations: 你还好意思说？ "How can you say that with a straight face?" |
| 自尊心 | Your pride, and how easily it bruises. Saying someone's 自尊心强 usually means they can't bear being looked down on, not that they feel good about themselves. | Pride that is easily hurt. 自尊心强 describes someone who can't stand being looked down on, not someone with high self-esteem. |
| 面子 | Social face: the standing other people grant you. You insist on paying the bill to give a friend 面子, and contradicting your boss in public makes him lose it. | Standing in other people's eyes. Paying the bill gives a friend 面子; contradicting a boss in public makes him lose it. |
| 内卷 | Pointless, exhausting competition where everyone works harder and nobody gets ahead. You feel it when classmates start studying at 5 a.m. only because others do. | Competition where everyone works harder and nobody gets ahead, as when a whole class starts studying at 5 a.m. because others do. |
| 饺子 | A crescent dumpling folded around a minced filling. You picture the whole family around the table on New Year's Eve, pinching edges, someone hiding a coin in one for luck. | The traditional New Year's Eve food in northern China, usually made together by the whole family. |
| 比较 | Fairly or rather, with nothing actually being compared. When you say 今天比较忙, you are gently saying it's pretty busy, a modest way to put it. | Usually just "fairly", with nothing compared: 今天比较忙, "I'm fairly busy today". It softens the statement. |
