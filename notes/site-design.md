# longyan.app design

The site should feel like the app: exact, calm, made for reading Chinese. Black pages for type and UI, and between them colour, like a Mac's wallpaper: sheets of the demo selection's own blue, violet and rose folded over a night ground. Down the page it warms, and the closing turns toward dawn.

## Color

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Night | `#000000` | Page background. |
| Ink | `#f5f5f7` | Headlines and body text on Night. |
| Graphite | `#86868b` | Secondary text, the grey half of captions. |
| Moonlight | `#a1bde3` → `#c2abe0` | The eye's colour as it comes in: blue and lavender, with rose and coral where the tint turns, like a pearl, cooling to white once each part is in. |
| Tone 1–4 | `#e39e94` `#9ecca8` `#a1bde3` `#c2abe0` | The app's dark-mode tone colors. Only the cards use them, where they mean something. |

The opening is night over the paper Chinese is learned on: a 田字格 practice grid, every square split by a dashed cross, lined up so the sentence sits exactly in a row of its squares, one character to a square. Hairlines, fading out away from the line, felt more than seen; once the sentence has flown into the eye the grid dims, so the eye stands on the night. try.js lines it up with the sentence, measured from where the characters sit in the layout (not where they're drawn as they rise in, which put it off and jumped it into place a second after load), and it fades in only once it's placed. Nothing glows and nothing moves. (Tried and dropped: night blocks, whose seams read as a glitch; rings round the eye, too plain; the page's folded sheets; the old waves of characters.) At rest the eye is white, like the mark in the bar, each character a little lighter or darker so it reads as print. The colour is in the moment of coming in: the characters flying down warm into the pearl hues (azure into violet, magenta and coral across the eye, fuller than elsewhere), each part of the eye arrives in its hue and cools to white, so the growing edge is coloured and what's done is white. (Before: the eye rested in pearl tints with white flashes as it came in; flipped so the finished mark is the logo.) (Tried and dropped: a bloom that followed the eye, which read as stock circles sliding around; a blue-violet horizon, decoration with no source; the sentence and eye in tone colours, which made the eye read as a word cloud; a blurred copy of the eye glowing under it, which read as a glow effect.)

Backdrops (`paper.js`, for the statement's card, the recording, the menu tile, the install screens and the closing) are a few sheets of colour folded over a deep night ground, the way macOS wallpapers are: each lit along its top edge, deepening below it, casting a soft shadow on the one behind, its colour drifting toward the next along its length. The palettes come from the selection's iridescent sheen (system blue, violet, rose), so the Liquid Glass over them has colour to catch: azure (blue to violet), cobalt (violet to magenta), sky (blue to cyan), indigo (violet to rose), and dawn for the closing (violet to coral). The top of each is kept deep, where text and the demo sentence sit. Sheets rise to the right in some backdrops and to the left in others, at angles of their own, with uneven edges, so no two look alike. Live backdrops drift slowly. Grain is a faint CSS overlay. Where words sit on a full-bleed backdrop (the recording's heading, the closing), a soft dark halo behind them keeps them readable over the brightest sheets.

## Type

- **SF Pro Display**, semibold (600), tight tracking, for headlines. Crisp rather than airy.
- **SF Pro Text** for body and UI. Captions lead with a bold phrase and continue in grey.
- **PingFang SC** for Chinese, everywhere.

## Layout

1. **Story.** Pinned while it plays (200vh; the gather takes 54vh of scrolling). The page opens on the demo sentence, so the first thing a visitor does is try the app. Over it, one line says what this is ("The free Chinese pop-up reader for Mac and Windows") and a smaller one says what to do ("Click any word or drag to select text.", "Tap" on touch screens). Its characters rise in one by one on load. The first frame is the opening: with the script on, the eye's headline starts put away (before, it showed for a moment on load and slid off). Nothing plays on its own: no automatic selection, no card until the visitor makes one. Selecting works as in any Mac text: drag across characters, double-click a word, triple-click the sentence, shift-click to extend. Let go and the app's card opens for what the app reads there, below the sentence when there's room, while the selection stays where it was made. One character is one character, and its card says which word it's part of (a click on that row opens the word's card in its place); a selection that cuts a word in half gets the card for the whole word, as the app widens it (scripts/site-data.py makes sure of it where the app missed one: 年回 read 年回家, splitting 过年). Every selection the sentence allows has the app's own answer. Nothing says the card can be dragged; people find it. A little after the first lookup (2.2 s), a button, "Get Longyan" (black, with a white outline, quieter than the white Download), ends the column under the sentence: a little below the card while it's open (the card stops short of it and scrolls inside, as the app's does). Beside it, at the same moment, a quieter "Try another sentence" (no outline, a turning-arrow icon) swaps in the next of four sentences: 最近工作太卷了，我想躺平。, 今天我请客，你们随便点。, 他上班摸鱼，被老板发现了。 and 过年回家，爷爷给了我一个大红包。 (slang, hospitality, slang, New Year, each with words that carry a note; a fifth, about 吃醋 over a boyfriend's likes, was dropped). The card goes and the new line rises in without the first one's shimmer; nothing behind it changes. All four sentences are set at the size the longest one fits at, and each sits on the same grid columns, so the grid never moves or fades. Every selection of all four was checked one by one against the real card it opens on the page, with each word row opened, three times over (scripts/site-data.py has the corrections). Until a meaning is picked by hand, a phrase card's word row says what the word means here, the same as its own card. The row has one place, two thirds of the way down (and at least 150px under the line), and only fades in; it never moves, and the card opens over it at full height if it reaches that far. "Picked from context" shows on the first card opened only; no later card has it. The button glides on to the eye in 2.9 s: it eases out of the line, holds an even pace through the middle so the words in flight are never rushed, and makes a long settle as the eye fills in and the headline rises. While a card covers the button's place (opening, open or fading out) the button steps out of the way, so a card is never behind it. On a phone a swipe on a card is always the card's: it scrolls the card when the card has more to show and stops at its ends, and never scrolls the page under it, which would put the card away mid-read (no scroll lock at the top: the line and the card never pass a swipe on, so the demo is only left by a swipe on the page). Scrolling does the same. As the visitor scrolls, the sentence comes apart at the original opening's pace, played backwards: each character lifts off at a moment of its own (a comma or full stop fading as the character before it lifts, so the line never shrinks before it breaks up) and takes a long, unhurried flight, its tint turning from the line's white to the eye's as it goes. They fly in arcs to their places in the Longyan eye, spread evenly over it and taken in the order they land, each well away from the ones already down, so the eye fills from all over at once and nothing piles up in one spot. Where each lands the eye blooms out from it, nearest first, the big characters a beat ahead of the small; round a character still in the air the eye waits for it, so none drops into a hole left for it, like the old opening played backwards. The last characters to land come down one at a time, never two together. The eye fills at one steady pace for the whole mark, from the first landing to the end, easing off but never stopping, so it never stalls between landings; each character only waits for one landing right beside it. A character lands at full size and the eye grows out from it: each new character opens out from a little smaller as it appears, never shrinking into place, and flashes white, until the whole mark stands. The eye is set in everyday sentences, in pearl tints, in characters of nine sizes. Every character is whole; nothing is masked or cut. On the outline a character may stand a hair past the edge, so the outline takes every size from fine to large and reads as type rather than a stencil. Pointing at a character of the eye shows the word it's part of and what it means in that sentence. On a wide screen the eye sits on the right and the words on the left, the two set as one unit centred on the screen: "Now do that in any app." if the visitor tried a word (one line on a phone, its type sized to fit) ("Longyan gives every word the meaning it has in context. It works in your browser, WeChat, your PDFs and every other app, even offline."), "Read Chinese in any app." if not. Under it, Download and a quiet "How it works" with a down arrow, which glides to the statement. On a phone or an upright tablet the eye is on top and the words centred under it.
2. **Statement.** Three lines, shown one at a time ("An ordinary dictionary would throw every meaning at you." with "every meaning" in the system purple / "Longyan shows you which one your sentence really means…" with just "one" in the checkmark's blue / "and teaches you the cultural context you need to know." with "cultural context" in green), beside the app's real word card for 躺平, on Liquid Glass over a small wallpaper (the same painted backdrop as the feature tiles), as it floats over a Mac's desktop. A small segmented switch under it, two symbols (sun, moon) with no words, quiet as a window's own controls, shows the card in dark (the default: its smoky tint; the wallpaper dims, as macOS's do) or light (a brighter frosted white, with the purple, blue and green washes a little stronger so they still read as colour on white); the choice is remembered on that device. The wallpaper is tall enough for the card at its fullest, so it never resizes as the card grows in the demo sentence, built into the page. The section is pinned for three stretches of scrolling, 60vh each, one per line; three short bars under the line fill as you scroll through each stretch, so you can see how far along you are. The next line only comes once you've scrolled to the end of the last one's stretch, and never sooner than 1.2 s after it, so each line and its card change settle first; the old line lifts away and the new one rises in. The card shows a little more with each line, in that line's colour, with one light behind the meanings: first just 躺平, its pinyin and both meanings, with one purple box over both a moment after it comes in ("every" is purple too); then "to opt out of the rat race" opens in under the word as the box draws in onto that meaning on the app's spring as it turns blue, the check springs in and the other meaning steps back; then the "Cultural context" note opens in green as the blue fades. The light is a plain wash with no outline, in purple because warm hues (yellow, orange) go muddy over the dark card; each colour is its own layer, so they cross-fade without a muddy mix. Until the note is in, the card can't be clicked, so nothing breaks the sequence; after that it works like the app (and going back up resets it). Nothing in it re-wraps or snaps: it hugs its content, the space above the meaning opens with it, the pick isn't set in bold, there's no note mark, and going back up closes things smoothly. The same care holds for the card in the try section: rows restyle in place, so a pick's check springs in while the old one fades, and "Back to …" and the captions open and close. The content is set from the top, so the card grows downward and the line never moves. Scrolling back up undoes it all in order. It works the same on a phone or an upright tablet, with the card under the line; any landscape screen over 900px keeps them side by side. Without motion the lines simply stack beside the whole card. A click on a meaning shows the whole card.
3. **Longyan works everywhere you read.** Full-bleed azure backdrop, its sheets set low so the heading sits on the night. The film fills its frame edge to edge: a Mac desk with a web article, an email and a PDF, and the app reading one thing in each (上火 in the article, 背锅 in the mail, the phrase 刀子嘴豆腐心 in the PDF), each card the app's own answer for that sentence. It's rendered frame by frame, not screen recorded, so the pointer moves on smooth curves and the camera follows on springs with no jumps; it starts and ends on the same wide shot so the loop has no seam. It sits with a screen's rounded corners and shadow, wider than the text column (up to 1280px) and sized so all of it fits in the window at once. It plays muted on a loop while it's on screen (HEVC, H.264 where there's no HEVC); without motion it shows its first frame with the usual controls.
4. **Made for Chinese language learners at every level.** "Skip the textbook dialogues and read real Chinese. Look up any word and learn it the way it's used right there." (Says what every level gets: real reading now, each word learned in its sentence. It no longer lists pinyin, slang or the dictionary's size, which the tiles and every dictionary already say.) Four tiles, each a piece of the real UI with a caption under it, landscape at every size (11:8, 4:3 on a phone) so each is a window onto the app, not a poster. Each tile has a technique of its own, the way photon.codes never repeats one in neighbouring pieces (a fan of glass, sky, flat colour with devices, soft gradients with frosted UI, a pattern with a wordmark, blocks and an arc), each made of what the tile says. Notes: the notes' own "Lost in translation" symbol at poster size, two speech bubbles cut from paper, an amber one with 文 and a pink one with A, on a deep coral, the one warm tile; a soft light inside each, as the blocks have it; the notes, white in the app's light appearance, sit where the two meet. Still; only the notes move, and a card's heading and text show only while it's in front. (Tried and dropped: a plain teal, too plain; rows of phrases with their word-for-word English struck through, drifting, which read as errors and fought the notes.) Tones: the one light tile, a mesh of the four tones' colours glowing like an iPhone wallpaper, one light behind each column at its tone's pitch, under the panel in the app's light appearance with the tones' light-mode inks. History: blue blocks on a spreadsheet's grid, every seam on a line of it. The menu: violet and orchid blocks with a pink arc. The UI is the app in its dark appearance (dark frosted material, a hairline rim, the dark tone colours), so the tiles belong to the page rather than reading as a set of bright cards dropped on it. As the grid scrolls in, each tile comes in in layers: it rises and opens out from a little smaller, its colour settles from a touch larger, and its UI lifts into place a beat later, then the caption; the right-hand tile of a row a beat after the left. Each tile does one thing, on a loop, only while on screen: real notes from the app come up one at a time, the two before stepping back behind, each headed as in the app (its SF Symbol, translate or globe.asia.australia.fill, in the note's colour, the label in grey). Both symbols, here and on every card and in the film, are the system's own semibold outlines traced at 20x (not redrawn), sized and centred as the app's 11 pt label sets them; the four tones are said in turn, each column lit in its colour while its pitch contour draws over the character; a new word arrives on top of History every few seconds, the list running off the foot of the tile and fading out there; the eye stands at the top right of its menu, with no menu bar, clock or taskbar, so it reads as either app's (menu bar on a Mac, notification area on Windows), and the highlight runs down it. Still with reduced motion.
5. **Trusted by expats, heritage speakers, HSK test-takers, and educators.** Its own band between the features and the steps: the sentence itself, selected. On a sky, the way Photon opens 2.0: saturated azure lightening downward, a violet glow in one corner (the eye's violet, so the daylight and the opening's light are one family), two long soft beams of light crossing high on the left; the page's one burst of daylight between the night sections. Each line is a selection block in frosted white (the selection's blue would vanish into the sky), with white handles, the white words carrying a faint shadow to hold on the pale patches, the selection's handles at the start and the end, and each reader in a white capsule, plain text at the line's own size and baseline (it never breaks across lines), led by a character in its tone's colour, in the Chinese face, then a hairline: 外 wài, 家 jiā, 考 kǎo, 教 jiào. Up to 50px, 28px on a phone. It rises in with the section, like the rest of the page; nothing else moves (a typed-in version was tried and dropped as distracting). No buttons; the steps right below have them.
6. **Installation.** Its own thing, not a fifth tile. Under the heading, the statement's segmented control, at its own size, picks Mac or Windows (a download button for one system picks it too, and the choice is remembered on the device; a Windows PC starts on Windows); only that system's download, small print and steps show. Mac: "Download for Mac". Windows: "Get it from the Microsoft Store", with "Installer on GitHub" beside it as a quiet link. Either way a screen on the black, a night wallpaper of the site's own colours drifting on it (indigo for the Mac, azure for Windows), playing the three steps as they happen. Mac: the zip opened in Finder and the app dragged to Applications; the "downloaded from the Internet" alert, and Open; the app's own first window (its real words), Accessibility switched on, and the reading model downloading. Each app's menu bar shows that app's menus. Windows, in Windows 11's light appearance and its own fonts where the visitor has them: the Store's page for the app, Get, a moment of downloading, Open; the app's welcome window in its real words, Download, the model downloading, Done; then the app's notification, the arrow by the clock opening the hidden icons, a right-click on the eye and its real menu. The pointer is the Mac's black arrow on the Mac and Windows' white one on Windows, and on Windows it aims at the buttons where the layout puts them. Beside it the three steps, the one playing lit with a bar filling as it plays (as the opening's bars do); click a step to see it. It plays only on screen and goes round again. On a phone the Mac's screen shows the middle of the desk, larger; the Windows desk narrows instead, so the taskbar and the clock stay whole. With reduced motion each step shows its scene as a still.
7. **Closing.** Full-bleed dawn backdrop, the eye mark in white with no tile around it, "Free + Offline + Open source" (the plus signs light and dimmer, always on one line: below its own size it scales with the screen), one plain line ("There's no account to make and nothing to pay. All the code is on GitHub."), the download button.

## Crispness

- The mark is drawn from its geometry, not traced: three nested eye shapes (each the hull of a circle and two points, half-widths 1, 5/8 and 3/14) and one wedge. The stroke is even all round and the edges are as sharp as the screen. The old PNG wobbled.
- Listen speaks with the best Mandarin voice the visitor's browser has, ranked as the app ranks them: Premium or Enhanced first, then Edge's natural and Chrome's Google voices, then Tingting; the novelty voices (Eddy, Grandma...) only if there's nothing else.
- The tab icon is the mark in white on a black rounded square that fills the icon (`images/tab-icon.svg`, with `favicon.ico` for browsers that want one). The app icon, with its margin and shadow, is too small to read at 16px. On iPhone the home-screen icon is the same, square and opaque (`images/touch-icon.png`); iOS rounds it.
- One material: characters. Everything in the hero is drawn in them, whole. No masks, no halo, no glow.
- The selection is the system blue with an iridescent violet-and-rose sheen moving through it, sized to the selection so even one character shows it.
- On a wide screen the eye is about as tall as the headline and the lines under it (38% of the window's height, at most 420px), with a gap of at most 110px between them.
- The bar's logo is the mark alone in white, the app icon's negative, 25px wide.
- Hairlines are 0.5px. Shadows are layered: a 0.5px ring, a tight shadow and a wide soft one.
- Boxes on the black page (the statement's wallpaper, the feature tiles, the Mac's bezel) get a light 1px rim drawn over their content, so a dark edge never melts into the page on a phone.

## Motion

- On load the sentence's characters rise in one by one, 40 ms apart. The backdrop drifts slowly.
- The story is scrubbed by scroll, smoothed a little each frame so a wheel step doesn't jump. The sentence turning into the eye takes 0.6 of a screen of scrolling. On the eye screen the headline rises line by line, then the line under it and the buttons. The card is the app's, with its springs: it scales in from the selection, its height follows its content, and rows slide open.
- Below the story, sections rise into place once, and the recording lifts out of its backdrop as it comes into view.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no flight, no rise, the hero drawn still. `?still` does the same for screenshots.
- The link preview (images/og-7.jpg, 2400×1260) is the eye screen as it stands, with Download and How it works: `?og` at 1200×630, 2x, no top bar. A new preview gets a new file name, since apps cache the old one by URL.

## The card

The demo card is the app's card, not a picture of it: same sizes, colors, Liquid Glass and motion, and the same answers. When the app's card changes, `card.js` changes with it. Pin works as it does in the app: a pinned card stays where it is, a new lookup replaces it in place, and only its close button or Esc closes it (it goes when the sentence does). The card built into the statement has no Pin.

## Words

Plain and short. Say what the app does in the visitor's words. No "seamless", "powerful", "unlock". Buttons say what happens: "Download for Mac", "Get it from the Microsoft Store".
